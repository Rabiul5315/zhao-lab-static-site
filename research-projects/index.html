Current Projects

FoodProt

Building a Comprehensive Food Protein Database (FoodProt) for Predictive Modeling of Protein Functionalities

Support

USDA-NIFA-AFRISouthern California Institute of Food Technologists Section (SCIFTS)

Collaborators

  • Dr. Pam Ismail, University of Minnesota
  • Dr. Yonghui Li, Kansas State University
  • Dr. Youling Xiong, University of Kentucky
  • Dr. Hualu Zhou, University of Georgia

This project aims to build a comprehensive, robust, and accessible food protein database, FoodProt, for predicting protein functionalities under various processing conditions. The specific objectives are to: (1) establish a structured database using literature data extracted with AI-supported tools; (2) enhance the database by integrating high-quality datasets generated through collaborative studies and molecular dynamics simulations; and (3) build a collaborative network through education and outreach activities.

Microalgae

Unlocking the Green Potential: Genome-Scale Metabolic Modeling for Microalgae-Based Food Innovation

Support

USDA-NIFA-AFRISDSU DRIUSDA-NIFA-NNFUSDA-NIFA-HEC

Collaborators

  • Dr. Changqi Liu, School of Exercise and Nutritional Sciences, SDSU
  • Dr. Cristal Zuniga, Department of Biology, SDSU

This study aims to harness genome-scale metabolic modeling to enhance the nutritional profiles, flavor characteristics, and production efficiency of microalgae. The long-term goal is to expand the applications of microalgae as a versatile food ingredient and alternative protein source. The specific objectives are to: (1) optimize and scale up microalgae nutrient and pigment production using genome-scale metabolic modeling; (2) improve the flavor profile and sensory properties of microalgae ingredients; (3) develop innovative microalgae-based food products; and (4) investigate the heavy-metal and allergen safety of microalgae.

Coffee Flavor

The Effect of UHT and Retort Processing on the Quality and Stability of Ready-to-Drink Coffee Beverages Containing Milk

Support

SCIFTSCalifornia Dairy Innovation Center

Collaborators

  • Dr. Anuradha Prakash, Project PI, Chapman University
  • Dr. Thuy Xuan Uyen Phan, Chapman University

The goal of this project is to evaluate differences in the physicochemical properties, sensory attributes, and flavor and aroma compounds of ready-to-drink coffee beverages containing milk that are sterilized using UHT or retort processing and evaluated during storage. The milk types include conventional milk and lactose-free milk.
